Project Overview

This project examines cargo transportation across East Africa using selected statistics from major transport corridors and ports. The project also evaluates the accessibility of a web page presenting cargo transportation information.

East African cargo transportation is important for connecting coastal ports with landlocked countries including Uganda, Rwanda and Burundi.

Week 2 Accessibility Audit

The following issues were identified when evaluating the accessibility of the cargo transportation website.

1. Missing alternative text

Who may be affected: Blind and visually impaired users.

Barrier: Screen readers cannot explain the information contained in images.

Improvement: Add meaningful alt text to informative images.

2. Poor colour contrast

Who may be affected: Users with low vision or colour-vision difficulties.

Barrier: Low-contrast text can be difficult to read.

Improvement: Use sufficient contrast between text and background.

3. Inaccessible tables

Who may be affected: Screen-reader users.

Barrier: Users may have difficulty understanding relationships between statistics and their headings.

Improvement: Use captions, table headers and the scope attribute.

4. Poor keyboard navigation

Who may be affected: Users with motor disabilities.

Barrier: Users who cannot operate a mouse may struggle to navigate the site.

Improvement: Make all links and controls keyboard accessible.

5. Small or fixed-size text

Who may be affected: Users with visual difficulties and older users.

Barrier: Small text makes statistics difficult to read.

Improvement: Use responsive text and allow users to zoom the page.

6. Unclear navigation

Who may be affected: Users with cognitive disabilities and first-time visitors.

Barrier: Users may have difficulty locating important information.

Improvement: Use clear, descriptive and consistent navigation links.

Week 2 Reflection

During the accessibility audit, I examined an East African cargo transportation website and identified several barriers that could prevent users from accessing information effectively. The major issues identified included missing alternative text, poor colour contrast, inaccessible tables, limited keyboard navigation, small text and unclear navigation. I addressed these problems by using semantic HTML, accessible navigation, table captions and headings, readable text sizes, keyboard-friendly links and responsive design. The project also helped me understand that accessibility should be considered from the beginning of website development rather than added at the end. Using cargo transportation statistics made the project practical because the information can be useful to logistics companies, researchers, students and policymakers across East Africa.
